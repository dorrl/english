# Cosmic Slugger

A classroom spectator game for 2–30 students. Paste one name per line and start the show: one giant cosmic swing launches every student's colored ball into a destructible galaxy. Rank by **survival + distance + additional points**: tiered survival rewards, 1 point per whole forward meter, 150 per destroyed planet, and signed special-event rewards, deductions or swap adjustments.

## Local development

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build
```

Vite serves the game at its configured base path (currently `/`). `npm run preview` previews the production build. The game needs WebGL; sound starts with the teacher's start/replay click. Everything runs in the browser without external assets, APIs, accounts, or a server runtime.

On entry, an optional animated six-step guide explains name entry, automatic flight, scoring, survival tiers, the original four special planets, and four new effects. Cards float, the sample ball flies toward a planet, survival tiers light up in sequence, and new icons demonstrate reversal, swapping and growth. Reduced-motion preferences disable animation. Skip or Escape closes any step; the topbar reopens it while idle. “Do not show again” is opt-in and browser-local (v3 presents the changed rules again). The native modal confines keyboard focus and returns it on close; storage restrictions do not prevent dismissal.

## Show structure

- The lower-left flight HUD shows the camera subject's live overall rank in a shield badge, name, score and forward distance in meters. Rank uses the same tie-breaks as the sidebar; top three badges use gold, silver and bronze. The badge animates on subject/rank changes, respects reduced motion, and uses a separate compact number row to keep the crown clear of the digits on phones.

- Special planets attract balls with twice the gravitational strength of equal-sized normal planets, within the same attraction range. The per-planet 40 m/s² and summed 55 m/s² acceleration caps still apply, along with each ball's permanent gravity reduction.

- Survival rewards count completed simulation seconds: 20 points/second for seconds 0–30, 30 for 30–45, 50 for 45–60, and 100 thereafter. Earlier tiers are not repriced (30s = 600; 45s = 1,050; 60s = 1,800). Finished balls stop accruing points. One-time “30초 경과”, “45초 경과” and “60초 경과” notices show the new rate and the gravity changes at 30/60 seconds. Sidebar, focus score and results all use the same total, with a separate survival breakdown in results.
- Four new special planets supplement the original four without increasing total special density. Mint light-gravity planets permanently multiply the ball's global and planetary gravity by 0.75 per hit (floor 0.35). Blue reversal planets reverse global vertical gravity for five simulation seconds (repeated hits refresh the duration). Peach growth planets multiply rendered size and collision radius by 1.25 (ceiling 2.2). New rounds reset all upgrades.
- Gold swap planets make up only 0.5% of specials (about 0.07% of all planets). A seeded active opponent exchanges position, distance record and exact current total with the actor; identities, velocities, survival clocks, destruction counts and upgrades stay with their owners. Signed event-point adjustments account for score components that stay with each student. No eligible opponent produces a clear no-target notice. Swaps reset trail history and invalidate old-position contacts rather than sweeping through intervening worlds. A gold glow, portal burst and dedicated sound highlight the event; its priority notice lasts 8 seconds on desktop / 6.5 on compact layouts, and finishes before results appear.

- A three-beat countdown leads into a loaded backswing, a brief brace, and an accelerating forward sweep driven from the grip pivot. The bat reaches the exact launch plane before hit-stop, then continues into a slowing, slightly raised follow-through and settles. Pose and velocity curves stay continuous between phases.
- Contact triggers a short hit-stop, synchronized swing whoosh and bass/crack, camera recoil, a glowing core, debris, and shock rings. A short bounded ribbon traces the barrel; the launch camera briefly holds the follow-through before tracking a ball.
- Automatic flight framing uses a live shortlist: the top 20% of active students by score plus students gaining points rapidly over the latest three seconds. Brief scoring bursts stay queued until a cut can show them. Qualified hot scorers get priority; visit counts rotate shots within the pool. Finished students leave immediately, and two-student rounds still alternate. Shots keep seeded random holds of 2.6–6.4 seconds (4–7.5 with reduced motion). Camera angles remain close and slightly elevated; transitions stay smooth and tracking adapts to boosted speed. Camera selection only reads scores and never changes physics.
- Off-screen planet impacts produce a quiet tremor and softer sound, without global flash, hit-stop, or camera redirection. Visible impacts retain full feedback.
- Audio separates a warm countdown, smooth rising charge, accelerating air sweep, layered bat crack/body, size-dependent planetary crumble and a soft winner chord. Impacts pan gently with their screen position; off-screen hits remain centered and quiet. Separate collision rate limits and six impact voices prevent a chain reaction from masking launch/reveal cues. Cached mono buffers, a 32 Hz high-pass and gentle master compression keep playback bounded; reset/tab hiding releases all voices.
- A 10,000 m map scatters 8,448 planets across a 288 m wide field (x ±144 m). Density is about 9.5% higher than the earlier 5 km / 172 m wide / 2,304-planet field, accounting for both added length and width. Every round changes positions, radii, colors, and rings using its seed. Free scatter and loose pockets replace regular rows; each of 32 sectors includes a guaranteed lower-field population just above the -115 m disappearance floor. A spatial hash enforces initial surface clearance. Small, medium, and large planets have radii from 1.6 to 6.8; large planets receive fewer rings to keep the flight readable. Existing meshes and shared surfaces are reused on reset. Hidden planet groups skip decorative matrix updates, and static child matrices are cached.
- About 14% of planets carry colored glowing orbits and icons: cyan speed (+50%, within a 900 m/s safety ceiling), gold score (+1,000), pink score wave (−500 to a seeded sample of about 20% of the other students, minimum one, including finishers), and violet magnet. Deductions can make scores negative. Magnet range starts at 28 m and stacks by 12 m to a 64 m ceiling, moving real planets toward the nearest eligible ball. Events resolve once per destroyed planet; pooled markers and a three-card queued upper-left feed identify the actor, effect, and affected count. Notification cards feature an animated emblem, large reward/penalty, 5.2-second lifetime rail, and a collapsing fade/slide exit. Reduced-motion settings remove animation. Late events get reading time before the winner reveal.
- Nearby planets exert softened inverse-square gravity in all three axes with a smooth range cutoff. Summed acceleration, forward velocity and total speed are bounded to avoid trapping, singular kicks and runaway collision boosts. Moving worlds use relative swept collision tests; cached depth buckets update as magnets move worlds. A fixed 120 Hz step, seeded targets and contact-order ownership preserve outcomes across frame rates. Global downward acceleration starts at 0.4914 m/s² (10% below 0.546), stays constant until 30 simulation seconds, then rises gently by 0.002 m/s² each second until 60 seconds. From 60 seconds it rises faster by 0.055 m/s² each second to a 3.2 m/s² ceiling. One-time priority notices announce both stages immediately above up to three queued student cards; replay resets the notices. The fallback timeout is 110 seconds so the ramp can take effect. Outside x ±100.8 m, a smooth, weak center pull ramps up to 0.65 m/s²; the inner flight corridor is unaffected.
- The cosmic bat uses a beveled lathed metal body, studio reflections, wrapped leather grip, segmented armor, exposed energy slots, an impact collar, and a vented end cap with individual bolts. Charge particles follow its moving sweet spot; the bat and every ball align on the impact plane at launch.
- Eight animated solar stars, sixteen satellites with solar panels and dishes, and four ring-shaped space stations provide visual landmarks around the flight field. These are scenery; planet scoring remains unchanged. Model parts and materials are reused, and off-screen landmarks are culled.
- Every round seeds different left/right and upward/downward launch angles, mixing sliders, vertical curves, knuckleball oscillations, and smooth late breaks. Initial launch speed is 6% faster with the same angle distribution. The forces stay continuous and bounded. A fixed-step simulation preserves the same outcome for a given seed across frame rates. Different launch angles produce different flight distances; reaching the map limit is not guaranteed.
- Live ranking, the focused student's score, and the results all use one scoring function. Cards show survival and net additional points instead of a raw destruction count; row tooltips include the full distance/planet/event breakdown. The winner gets survival points and time alongside distance, planet points and event/swap adjustments. Equal totals favor more planets, then distance, then shorter flight time and stable input order.
- The ranking sidebar uses individual score cards, gold/silver/bronze rank badges, student-color score bars relative to the leader, a camera-focus outline, finish checkmarks, and a completion rail. Continuous position changes include brief direction arrows; reduced motion skips movement. Narrow screens use a compact, scrollable board below the arena.
- Device layouts live in `src/device-layout.css`. Tablets (600–1,366 px) use the desktop composition in both orientations: full-height arena, right-hand scrolling scoreboard and up to three normal notification cards stacked over the upper-left arena, with separate priority notices. No tablet notification rail is reserved. Sidebar width adapts to the available screen; touch controls remain at least 44 px and roster text stays 16 px to avoid input zoom. Phones below 600 px retain the lower scoreboard and one compact 3.4-second notification in a 58 px rail below the arena. Layout changes preserve queued events. Dynamic viewport sizing and safe-area padding support rotation.
- Planet destruction uses pooled effects (600 fragments, 24 rings, 8 cores) and shared planet textures/materials. Off-screen worlds are culled.
- After all flights finish, a brief suspense beat leads to the winner and top three. Replay preserves the roster; reset returns to name editing, including during countdown or charging.
- Reduced-motion preferences soften flashes and camera shake and disable speed lines.

## Source structure

- `src/main.ts`: round state machine, camera, rendering, and UI.
- `src/tutorial.ts`: skippable entry guide, score example, keyboard dismissal and optional local preference.
- `src/special-events.ts`: special-planet visual identity, notification descriptions and bounded on-screen event cards.
- `src/camera-director.ts`: score/rate-based waiting pool, fair rotation inside that pool and seeded camera shot timing.
- `src/arena.ts`: generated galaxy, shared surfaces, launch platform, bat, and balls.
- `src/bat.ts`: detailed procedural bat geometry, reflective finishes, grip texture, energy core, and reusable swing trail.
- `src/bat-motion.ts`: shared motion timing and continuous bat pose curves, including the exact contact pose.
- `src/scenery.ts` and `src/scenery-layout.ts`: generated solar-star shaders, satellite/space-station models, bounded placement, animation, and culling.
- `src/effects.ts`: bounded reusable visual effects.
- `src/audio.ts` and `src/sound-design.ts`: cached procedural audio, impact budget, panning, mixing and source cleanup.
- `src/simulation.ts`: deterministic 120 Hz ballistic flight, swept collision detection, and ranking. Presentation time does not change scores.
- `src/presentation.ts`: on-screen/off-screen impact feedback strengths.
- `src/ranking.ts`: continuous ranking animation, including updates received during a move.
- `src/style.css`: responsive arena and scoreboard, with a separate bottom scoreboard on small screens.
- `scripts/simulation.test.mjs`: bat contact direction, phase continuity and deck clearance, seeded planet placement and size variety, lower-field coverage and lateral spread, scenery bounds, camera rotation, field clearance and length, score calculation, mixed seeded launch variation, curve forces, off-screen feedback, input limits, one launch per student, collision tunneling, contested planets, ranking, and full 2/30-student rounds on randomized maps at different frame rates and with pauses.
- `scripts/audio.test.mjs`: cue levels and endpoints at common sample rates, transient/charge balance, pitch/pan bounds, near/far priority, dense collision playback, cached buffers and complete reset cleanup.
- `scripts/events.test.mjs`: special distribution, gravity direction/core/range stability, fly-by curvature, force bounds, exact event rewards, unique seeded penalty targets, stacking magnet range, moving-world collision/index updates and one-time resolution.

Every planet awards credit once, to the earliest contact. Names are trimmed, blank lines ignored, and the first thirty used. Student names are rendered as literal text.
